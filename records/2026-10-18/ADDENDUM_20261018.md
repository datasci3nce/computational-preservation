# Versioned addendum — output-informed interventions and cross-architecture validity (18 October 2026)

**Status:** Addendum to exploratory selected-case NCA research note. Based on completed experiments; original paper narrative and its evidence/limitations are **unchanged**. Full audit: [`../FREEZE_20261018.md`](../FREEZE_20261018.md).

## New selected-NCA connection

In the already inspected seven-cell seed-4/5 NCA, we distilled a previously successful **binary-only**, fold-directed bias-patched teacher into different parameter subsets of the original damaged model. The ordinary teacher outputs, not the task labels, were the student targets. At t512 on one 48-triplet standard-noise bank, recall for readout-only, input+bias, recurrence and full t128 students was **0/48**, **48/48**, **42/48** and **47/48** respectively. **All strict three-way recall remained 0/48**.

The student updates gave an opportunity to test a forward mechanistic prediction: use the old fold's critical left null vector to predict how a *small fraction* of each newly learned parameter update changes the tracked saddle-node location, **before running the augmented nonlinear fold solve**. At 10% update the predictions closely matched solved fold positions for input+bias, recurrence and full-t128 updates. The full paths moved the tracked fold across the operating region in successful students, while readout-only left it unchanged. New cue histories showed a graded increase in binary memory with update strength, not a sharp on/off threshold at the crossing.

An exploratory, retrospectively designed **fold-matched bias alternative** produced broadly similar but not universally identical recall to the learned updates. Fold geometry is thus a useful explanatory coordinate for this particular model, **not proven sufficient, unique, necessary across every allowed intervention, or generalizable to an independently trained NCA**. An ordinary task-gradient direction remained competitive in the earlier study. These conclusions should appear alongside the no-cue, noise, and selected-model limitations rather than replacing them.

## Complementary but separate new results

- **GRU:** same-task ensemble outputs can teach a merged student to recover delayed recall; the position of supervision in the sequence matters. New independent training seeds reproduce a t512 benefit but expose severe t1024 retention failure. These do not identify a unique causal mechanism from outputs.
- **Planted-fault GRU:** different causes can yield the same strict recall score; known readout, input and recurrence perturbations can be distinguished by hidden-state and restricted-repair probes. Blind unknown-fault mechanism diagnosis is not demonstrated.
- **ANN thesis pilot:** Homing-teacher distillation can restore target-light-sensitive steering on held-out observed sensors, but neither saved teacher nor distilled student reliably reaches the target. The old thesis-composite success and the checkpoint lineage remain unresolved. Neuron suppression influences light-sensitive steering, not a proven separate Exploration/Homing circuit.

## Explicit non-claims

This addendum does not claim (1) recovered original weights from scalar outputs, (2) inference of a probabilistic causal world model, (3) separation of complementary skills, (4) unique mechanism or invariant Homing neuron, (5) new symmetry mathematics, (6) NCA fold mechanism replicated in GRUs, (7) safety circuit preservation in LLMs, or (8) a general repair method superior to ordinary baselines.
